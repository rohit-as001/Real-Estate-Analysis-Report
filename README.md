# Real-Estate-Analysis-Report
### This Repository has Real Estate Analysis Report.

_"Location, Property, Performance — Turning Australian Real Estate Data Into Smarter Business Decisions"_


## Project Overview
Best Homes Real Estate Analysis Report is a comprehensive property sales analytics project built entirely in Microsoft Excel for Best Homes — an Australian real estate company. The project analyzes 6,000 listed properties across 4 major Australian cities, tracking sales performance, cost structure, other expense impact, monthly sales trends, salesperson contributions, and property type revenue breakdown over a full fiscal year (April – March).
The workbook features two fully interactive dashboard views — a main Analytics Dashboard for operational sales tracking and a Key Insights Dashboard that delivers executive-level narrative summaries with pre-built analysis blocks. Both dashboards are powered by a Month slicer and a Location slicer, enabling dynamic monthly and city-level filtering.
This analysis is directly relevant to real-world business needs in real estate portfolio management, sales team performance optimization, expense audit and cost control, location-based investment planning, and fiscal year financial reporting.


## Business Objectives
  - Monitor overall real estate sales performance
  - Analyze property demand by location and property type
  - Track revenue, cost, and net profit/loss
  - Identify top-performing salespersons
  - Understand monthly sales trends
  - Improve pricing, investment, and sales strategies

# Key Insights
1) 3 Bedroom Properties Drive the Highest Revenue at 47%
  - 3 Bedroom properties generate $117.48M (47%) of total revenue — the dominant product type by a significant margin — followed by 2 Bedrooms at $83.04M (33%) and 1 Bedroom at $51.65M (20%). Despite having fewer units sold (1,320 vs 1,565 for 1BR), the higher selling price of $89,000 per 3BR unit makes it the most financially impactful property type. This clearly signals where revenue concentration lies.


2) Adelaide Is the Top Performing City
  - Adelaide leads all cities in both sales volume (1,378 units) and total sales revenue ($82.84M). This is followed closely by Sydney ($78.69M / 1,310 units) and Melbourne ($77.86M / 1,296 units). Canberra is a significant outlier — with only 285 units sold and $12.77M in revenue — representing just 5% of total sales. The distance factor (58 mins from central Canberra vs. 11 mins for Adelaide) likely contributes to lower sales velocity there.


3) December Is the Peak Sales Month — and the Highest Loss Month
  - December records the highest monthly sales at $26.17M but simultaneously has the highest monthly loss at $68,431. This paradox reveals a critical business reality: higher sales volume in December drives proportionally higher Other Expenses, resulting in the largest loss in any month. January, conversely, records the lowest sales ($11.13M) and the lowest loss ($32,794) — consistent with a slower post-holiday market.


4) Net Loss Driven by Other Expenses — The Core Business Problem (Other Expenses Are Uniformly High Across All Dimensions)
  - Despite generating $252M in revenue, the company reports a net loss of $667,037. The root cause is Other Expenses of $94.6M — which consistently appear across all locations, property types, and months. With costs at $157M and revenue at $252M, the implied gross profit is approximately $95M. However, the $94.6M in Other Expenses nearly entirely eliminates this margin, leaving only a razor-thin $667K loss. Reducing or controlling Other Expenses is the single most impactful lever for profitability.
  - Other Expenses per transaction are: $14,895 per 1BR unit, $28,797 per 2BR unit, and $23,832 per 3BR unit (for single-quantity transactions). These are remarkably high relative to selling prices ($33K, $60K, $89K respectively), representing 45%, 48%, and 27% of the selling price in Other Expenses alone. The 2BR property type carries the proportionally heaviest expense burden relative to its revenue.


5) Jayson Chandler Leads All Salespeople at $94M
  - Jayson Chandler generates $93.53M in total sales — more than Virginia Senior ($73.13M), Enid Maddox ($60.19M), and Nia Gallegos ($25.32M) combined at approximately 37% of total revenue. This extreme revenue concentration in one salesperson is both a business asset and a risk. The gap between Chandler and Nia Gallegos (3.7× difference) indicates highly unequal sales team productivity that needs to be addressed.


6) Sales Growth Rate: 3BR at 47%, 1BR at Only 20%
  - The Key Insights view confirms that 3 Bedroom properties have the highest sales growth contribution at 47% while 1 Bedroom properties have the lowest at 20%. This growth differential should inform both inventory acquisition strategy (invest more in 3BR stock) and pricing review (explore if 1BR is optimally priced for its market segment).


7) August Is a Strong Mid-Year Revenue Month
  - The monthly trend shows that August generates $25.16M — the second-highest monthly revenue after December ($26.17M). May ($21.43M), October ($23.65M), and September ($23.30M) also perform well. The mid-year (August–October) window appears to be a consistent secondary revenue peak, suggesting a bi-modal sales cycle with peaks in August and December.



# Business Recommendations
1) Improve Profitability Management - (Although revenue is strong, the business still reports a net loss.)
  - Analyze operational expenses
  - Optimize pricing strategies
  - Reduce unnecessary overhead costs
  - Improve margin tracking


2) Focus on High-Demand Properties - (3-bedroom properties generate the highest revenue.)
  - Increase inventory for larger properties
  - Launch family-focused marketing campaigns
  - Prioritize premium housing developments


3) Optimize Underperforming Locations - (Canberra contributes significantly lower sales.)
  - Evaluate local market conditions
  - Improve promotional activities
  - Review pricing competitiveness
  - Analyze customer demand patterns


4) Replicate Top Salesperson Strategies - (Top-performing salespersons contribute a large share of revenue.)
  - Benchmark sales techniques
  - Provide training programs
  - Introduce incentive-based performance systems


5) Monitor Monthly Cost Trends - (Fluctuating losses indicate possible financial inefficiencies.)
  - Build monthly financial monitoring systems
  - Forecast seasonal demand
  - Align inventory and operational planning with market trends
